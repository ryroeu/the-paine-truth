The Paine Truth publishes long-form essays on history, civic life, and political thought. The premise is simple. When a public question feels new, it’s worth looking at how people faced a version of it before, what they could see from where they stood, and what they missed.

## Why the name?

It’s a play on Thomas Paine’s *Common Sense* and the phrase “the plain truth.” The pamphlet tradition is an invitation to write clearly about public matters and to give readers enough evidence to argue back.

## How the essays are sourced

The essays draw on historical evidence and distinguish the record from the author’s interpretation. Endnotes point to the material behind a claim, including primary documents where available and scholarship that helps put them in context.
